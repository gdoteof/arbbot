# Registry Rules Audit

Equivalence caveats in `config/registry.yaml` are only as good as the text they
were read from. This file records audits that went back to **both venues'
authoritative rule text** and says which recorded caveats survived.

**Rule: never vet or un-vet a cross-venue pair from a price crossing. Read
`rules_primary` + `rules_secondary` on Kalshi and the market `description` on
PM US, and record the audit here.**

Sources (both public, unauthenticated, read-only):

```
Kalshi  GET https://api.elections.kalshi.com/trade-api/v2/markets?tickers=<T1,T2,...>
        -> .markets[].rules_primary / .rules_secondary / .yes_sub_title / .close_time
PM US   GET https://gateway.polymarket.us/v1/markets?slug=<slug>
        -> .markets[0].description / .endDate
        (urllib gets 403; curl works. /events?slug= does NOT resolve these -
         use /markets?slug=.)
```

## 2026-08-05 — `SKIP_FAMILIES` re-check (bestai, aliens, gpt6, btcmax, btc150k)

**Why:** the caveats on these families were generated by
`scripts/gen_pmus_pairs.py`, which matched Kalshi's `yes_sub_title` (a display
label) rather than `rules_primary` (the contract). That method produces caveats
that describe the UI, not the rule. Four of the five families were excluded from
make-take scanning by `SKIP_FAMILIES` in `scripts/scan_maketake_dirs.py` on the
strength of those caveats.

**Headline: the generated caveats were wrong more often than they were right.**
Two families were excluded for reasons that do not exist; one is excluded for a
reason nobody had written down; two were roughly correct but understated.

### `bestai-26dec` (8 rels) — both recorded caveats were FALSE

| | Kalshi `KXLLM1-26DEC31` | PM US `aimrc-company1-2026-12-31` |
|---|---|---|
| Resolves on | "If **Google** has the top-ranked LLM" | "if **Google** has the #1 ranked AI model" |
| Style control | "check the **'Remove Style Control'** toggle" | "**(Style Control Off)**" |
| Source | Arena leaderboard | `arena.ai/leaderboard/text/overall-no-style-control` |
| Field | 8 companies, complete partition | same 8, complete partition |
| Tiebreak | Rank (UB) -> Score -> votes -> earlier release | Rank -> Score -> alphabetical |
| Measured | "on Dec 31, 2026"; close 10:00 AM ET | 12:00 PM ET Dec 31 |

- *"Kalshi markets are MODEL-named, PM US COMPANY-named (alias-mapped)"* —
  **FALSE.** Kalshi's rule is company-level, identical to PM US. `Gemini` /
  `Claude` / `Grok` / `Ernie` are `yes_sub_title` display labels. The alias map
  in `gen_pmus_pairs.py:70` is cosmetic. This caveat was read off the subtitle.
- *"Snapshot time / style-control setting must match"* — **style control
  MATCHES**, explicitly, on both sides, against the same leaderboard URL.
- **Surviving residual:** snapshot instants differ ~2h (PM US 12:00 PM ET;
  Kalshi states only "on Dec 31, 2026" and closes 10:00 AM ET — the 10:00 is
  *inferred from `close_time`*, not stated in the rule). Symmetric timing risk,
  not a directional criteria divergence.
- Tiebreak chains diverge only after an exact unrounded Score tie: unreachable.

**But the economics say don't trade it anyway.** Because both venues resolve a
complete 8-way partition with exactly one winner, holding all 8 in one direction
is *oracle-independent*: all 8 Kalshi YES pays exactly $1, all 8 PM US NO pays
exactly $7, total $8 regardless of what either venue decides. At 2026-08-05
prices that riskless combination costs **8.021 — 2.1c underwater**. Only
cherry-picked subsets cross, and cherry-picking is precisely what re-introduces
the divergence exposure. Best single leg (google) nets ~3.7c after fees =
**~8.6%/yr over ~165 days**, below the 12% soft unwind floor, against
`time-poty-26` running ~12% on the same class cap.

Also observed: **every PM US leg was bid-only** (`asks: []`) with very large
resting size (openai 63k @ 0.12, baidu 128k @ 0.02, xai 101k @ 0.01). The PM US
"price" on these is a one-sided quote, so any PM-US-derived mid is fictional and
the apparent cross-venue disagreement is largely that artifact.

### `aliens-26` (1 PM US rel) — recorded caveat was FALSE

*"Which officials qualify as 'confirmation' is worded differently"* — **FALSE.**
The qualifying-official sentences are near-verbatim identical, and **both**
venues expand "Cabinet" to the same enumerated list: the 15 department heads
plus EPA Administrator, WH Chief of Staff, DNI, OMB, CIA, USTR, UN Ambassador,
CEA Chair, SBA, OSTP. Deadlines are the same instant (Kalshi "before Jan 1,
2027" == PM US "by December 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET").

Surviving residual: each venue independently judges whether a statement was
"definitive". That is the same residual already recorded on the INTL twin
`xv-aliens-confirmed-before-2027` — whose vetting got this pair right, which is
itself evidence that the *generated* PM US caveats are the unreliable ones.

### `gpt6-ladder` (4 rels) — recorded caveat wrong, real divergence WORSE

*"'Release' definitions (beta/limited access) may diverge"* — wrong on its
stated point. The release definitions substantially agree (both admit paid
tiers and open beta, both exclude closed beta / enterprise-only).

The **real** divergence is the qualifying-model clause, and nobody had written
it down:

- Kalshi: a model **"called GPT-6 or greater"** — a pure name test.
- PM US: that **OR** *"any model that is identified by OpenAI as a new model
  generation successor to the GPT-5 generation"*, explicitly excluding
  GPT-5.7/5.8 decimals.

PM US is a strict **superset** of Kalshi: a differently-named GPT-5 successor
resolves PM US Yes and Kalshi No. So `P(PM US yes) >= P(Kalshi yes)` by
construction, PM US should price at or above Kalshi permanently, and a
(Kalshi YES + PM US NO) basket is short exactly that gap. **This family will
show a persistent structural "edge" that is not an edge.** Keep it excluded;
do not vet it on a crossing. Deadlines do match to the instant.

### `btcmax-26` (3 agent-vetted rungs) — understated

Recorded caveat is the window start (market creation vs Jan 1). Two things it
missed:

1. **Measurement method diverges.** PM US settles on a BRTI trimmed mean *"over
   any sixty second period"* — an any-point trigger. Kalshi's `rules_secondary`
   describes a trimmed mean over *"all BRTI values for each minute from market
   issuance until the specified time"* — a whole-period average, a far harder
   condition — and then in the same paragraph says a crossing *"at any point
   during the measurement period"* triggers immediate resolution. **Kalshi
   contradicts itself.** Intent is probably any-point (matching PM US), but that
   is not established from the text.
2. **The window start is ambiguous inside Kalshi alone** — `rules_primary` says
   the window starts `01/01/2026 12:00 AM`, `rules_secondary` says "from market
   issuance".

Added to `100k` / `110k` / `120k` only. The `130k`+ rungs are human-vetted on
the reasoning that a never-approached threshold moots both window and method;
that argument weakens as the strike approaches spot, which is exactly where
these three sit.

### `btc150k-ladder` (4 rels) — no change needed

The measurement language actually **matches**: both venues settle on a BRTI
trimmed mean over any sixty-second period excluding the top and bottom 20%. The
existing window-start caveat is correct and already human-vetted. Noted only
because Kalshi's `rules_primary` on this series is an **unfilled template** —
`"If the price of Bitcoin is above || Count || by || Date || at || Time ||"` —
so the terms have to be read from the title and `rules_secondary`.

## What was changed

`config/registry.yaml` only (gitignored — the vetted pair set stays private),
16 relationships, **caveats and `rule_citations` text only**. No `vetted_by` or
`vetted_at` field was touched and `config/tradable.yaml` was not modified, so
nothing became tradable: `human_vetted` stayed at 32 before and after. Backup at
`config/registry.yaml.bak-pre-rules-recheck`.

`scripts/scan_maketake_dirs.py` (`SKIP_FAMILIES`) was **deliberately not
edited** — Python is feature-frozen (`docs/parity-pins.md`, Geoff 2026-07-24),
the script is run by no systemd unit, and the Rust engine never reads it. Its
`bestai` and `aliens` entries are now known to rest on disproven caveats; its
`gpt6` and `btcmax` entries are correct for reasons it does not state. Left
stale on purpose rather than editing frozen code for no live effect.
